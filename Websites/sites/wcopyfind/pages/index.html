What WCopyfind does

Compares every pair

Give it a class set of papers and it compares each paper with every other one, and with any sources or past papers you add. Hundreds of documents take seconds.

Sees through small edits

Copied passages are found even when the copier changed a word here and there, and WCopyfind shows which words differ.

Shows the evidence

Each matching pair opens side by side in your browser, with every shared passage highlighted in both documents. Click one to jump to its twin.

How it works, in three steps

  1. Choose the documents. Drag a folder of papers into the New documents list. They'll be compared with each other. Put sources, past years' papers, or saved web pages in the Old documents list; they'll be compared only with the new ones.
  2. Decide what counts as a match. The shortest phrase to match (six words is a good start), how many shared words make a pair worth reporting, and whether to allow a few changed words.
  3. Compare. Pairs that share enough text appear as they're found. Double-click one to see the two documents side by side.

Who uses WCopyfind

  • Teachers and professors checking term papers, lab reports, and take-home exams for copying between students and from sources
  • Honor and conduct officers documenting suspected plagiarism clearly and fairly
  • Researchers tracing how language moves: between news services, from lobbyists into legislation, through scholarly literature
  • Editors and publishers comparing drafts, versions, and submissions
  • Anyone who needs to know how much text two or two thousand documents have in common

Interesting things WCopyfind can do →

Good to know

  • Reads Word (.docx and .doc), plain text, web pages, and PDFs that contain text
  • Works with any language that puts spaces between words
  • Compares the documents you give it; it doesn't search the internet (you can add web pages, though)
  • Free for any use, and open source under the GNU GPL
  • Runs on 64-bit Windows 10 and 11; Mac and Linux users can use Wine
