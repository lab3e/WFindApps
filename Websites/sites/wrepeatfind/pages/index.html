Repetition sneaks in

When you're writing fast, revising hard, or assembling a long document from many drafts, it's easy to leave a sentence in two places. Readers notice; spell checkers and grammar checkers don't. WRepeatfind does.

Finds real repeats

Not single words, but whole phrases, sentences, and paragraphs: six words or more by default, so ordinary phrases don't clutter the results.

Sees through edits

A repeated passage is found even if you changed a word or two in one copy. The differing words are shown in italics.

Checks whole books

Add your chapters in order and WRepeatfind checks them as one work, catching the passage in chapter 2 that turns up again in chapter 9.

Three steps

  1. Choose what to check. Add a document, drag in a folder of chapters, or paste text straight from your web editor or email.
  2. Decide what counts as a repeat. The defaults (six words or more, allowing two differing words) suit most writing.
  3. Find Repeats. Review the list, then open the report to see each repeat in context and fix or dismiss it.

Tips for writers and editors →

Good to know

  • Reads Word (.docx and .doc), plain text, web pages, and PDFs that contain text, or text you paste in
  • Works with any language that puts spaces between words
  • Remembers the repeats you dismiss, even after you edit and check again
  • Free for any use, and open source under the GNU GPL
  • Runs on 64-bit Windows 10 and 11; Mac users can try Wine
